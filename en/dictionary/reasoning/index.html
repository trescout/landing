What is Reasoning?

It is the ability of artificial intelligence to draw conclusions step by step by passing the information given to it through a logical filter, rather than just based on memorization.

Overview

This concept, also known as reasoning, is when artificial intelligence models use gradual thinking processes, just like a human, instead of producing a direct answer when solving complex problems. Thanks to this capability, the system analyzes the data it has, makes logical inferences, and reaches the right decision by evaluating possible outcomes. It enables models to provide much more reliable results, especially in areas that require deep thinking such as mathematics, coding and strategic planning.

Analogy: You can compare this to a student who tries to find a solution by writing the formulas one by one on a blank sheet of paper, instead of guessing the options as soon as he sees the question in a multiple-choice exam.

How it works

Artificial intelligence models create an internal chain of thought when reasoning. He divides the question posed to him into small sub-parts, solves each part in turn, and proceeds by checking the intermediate steps he produces. If he realizes that he made a mistake in a step, he corrects that step and draws a new road map to reach the final answer.

Where it is used

It is used to debug complex software errors, solve advanced mathematical problems, and analyze multi-stage strategic business decisions. In addition, this ability is also used in specialties such as medicine and law, where the margin of error must be very low.

Commonly confused with

It is often confused with simple pattern matching or retrieving information by rote. While pattern matching only finds similarities in historical data, reasoning produces new solutions within the framework of logic rules in new and complex situations that have never been encountered before.

Frequently asked questions

Does artificial intelligence really think like humans?

No. Artificial intelligence does not have biological consciousness. However, it imitates human thought structure very successfully by proceeding step by step within the framework of mathematical probabilities and logical rules.

Does every artificial intelligence model have the ability to reason?

No. Traditional models are generally designed to produce quick and direct responses. The ability to reason is found in a new generation of advanced models specifically trained for this process.
