# What is Click to Cancel?

*Dev · Last updated: October 12, 2026*

It is a regulatory rule that mandates subscription cancellations can be made easily with a single click, without complex processes.

## Overview

"Click to Cancel" is a legal and design standard that requires consumer subscriptions to be canceled as easily as they were started, without wearing out the consumer with unnecessary steps. It was enacted, particularly by the U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC), to prevent dark patterns. It prohibits companies from forcing users to call customer service or navigate complex menu labyrinths for cancellation.

***Analogy:** It is like being able to press a single button at the entrance and leave when you want to cancel your membership, instead of being asked for notarized documents after signing up for a gym at the door with a single signature.*

## How it works

System administrators place a prominent and directly accessible cancellation button on their web and mobile panels. When the user clicks this button, the system confirms the action without putting mandatory live support chats or misleading offer windows in between. The subscription termination process is completed in a single step by notifying the billing provider in the background.

## Where it is used

It is applied in SaaS software, digital media platforms, and e-commerce subscription systems' user experience and legal compliance processes.

## Commonly confused with

It is different from an ordinary cancellation interface; it is a strict regulation backed by official legal sanctions and deterrent fines.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is offering a discount to the user during cancellation against this rule?**

As long as it does not prolong or complicate the cancellation process, a single informative offer can be presented; however, the user's right to cancel directly cannot be blocked.

**Does this rule apply only to mobile applications?**

No; it covers websites, mobile applications, and all types of digital subscriptions initiated via telephone.
