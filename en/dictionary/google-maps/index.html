What is Google Maps?

It is a web-based digital map platform offering location, satellite imagery, live traffic information, and route planning.

Overview

Google Maps is a comprehensive mapping service that brings together geographical data, street views, business details, and transportation networks worldwide. It performs real-time traffic analysis to help users reach from one point to another in the fastest and most efficient way. At the same time, through its API services, it enables third-party applications to develop map and location-based features.

Analogy: It is like carrying a living globe in your pocket that instantly whispers every street in the world, open shops, and the current traffic conditions.

How it works

Satellite imagery, aerial photographs, and anonymous GPS data from user devices are processed in massive data centers. This data is transformed into map layers, and thanks to AI-powered algorithms, the most optimal driving, walking, or public transit routes are calculated in milliseconds.

Where it is used

It is used in daily directions, courier and logistics management, local business searches, and location-based mobile applications.

Commonly confused with

Unlike open-source and community-driven mapping projects such as OpenStreetMap, it is a proprietary, closed-source, and commercial ecosystem owned by Google.

Frequently asked questions

How does Google Maps know traffic congestion in real time?

It detects this by anonymously analyzing the GPS location and speed data of hundreds of thousands of active smartphones moving on the roads.

Can developers embed Google Maps into their own websites?

Yes. Through Google Maps API services, maps, route calculation, and place search features can be integrated into custom software.
