# What is Preprints?

*Dictionary · AI · Last updated: October 8, 2026*

Draft studies that have not yet undergone scientific peer review, which researchers publish to share their results quickly.

## Overview

Preprints are the initial versions of academic papers before they go through formal peer review processes and detailed examinations. Instead of waiting for approval processes that take months, scientists share their new discoveries rapidly on these platforms. This creates an environment for early discussion and feedback within the community.

***Analogy:** It is like the first draft copy an author reproduces to have close friends read before officially publishing their book.*

## How it works

Researchers upload their work to arXiv or similar open archives, where the texts become instantly readable by the whole world.

## Where it is used

They are used extensively in the academic world, especially in rapidly developing research fields such as artificial intelligence and medicine.

## Commonly confused with

They should not be confused with final articles in peer-reviewed journals; these are drafts that have not yet received full approval.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are preprints reliable?**

They are great for scientific discussion, but since they have not yet passed through a formal filter, they should be examined carefully before being taken as definitive proof.
