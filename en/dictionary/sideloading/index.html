What is Sideloading?

It is the method of installing applications on your device directly via files, outside of official app stores.

Overview

It refers to installing software on a device using installation files found on the internet that are not normally downloaded from the phone or computer's own store (such as the App Store or Google Play). This process gives you more freedom, but it can bring security risks as it falls outside of official oversight mechanisms.

Analogy: It is similar to going to a friend's house to pick up a handmade product directly instead of buying a packaged product from an official store.

How it works

You enable the 'allow unknown sources' option in your device's settings, then complete the process by running the installation file (e.g., an APK or IPA file) you downloaded from the internet.

Where it is used

On Android phones, it is used when you want to install custom software or try out applications that developers are still testing.

Commonly confused with

It should not be confused with standard downloads made through an app store.

Frequently asked questions

Is sideloading safe?

Not always; because you may not have passed security checks such as virus scanning offered by official stores.

Why is sideloading done?

It is preferred to access applications not available in official stores or to bypass restrictions.
