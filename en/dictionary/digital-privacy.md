# What is Digital Privacy?

Digital Privacy is the right of individuals to control and limit who can collect, store, and process the personal data they generate on the internet, smart devices, and digital services.

## 1. Etymological origin and basic definition: What does digital privacy mean?
The concept of privacy is derived from the Latin word "privatus," which means "not belonging to the public, separated from the community, personal, and isolated." In modern legal literature, it was first formulated in 1890 as "The Right to be Let Alone" in a historical article written by American jurists Samuel Warren and Louis Brandeis.

## The difference between cybersecurity and digital privacy
Cybersecurity is the armor that prevents your data from being stolen by unauthorized attackers (hackers) (the steel door and alarm system of the house). Digital privacy, on the other hand, is the right that guarantees guests legally entering your home (the apps and service providers you use) do not rummage through your drawers and sell your private notes to others.

## Frequently asked questions
**What does Digital Privacy mean, and what is its Turkish equivalent?**
Digital Privacy is translated into Turkish as "dijital gizlilik" or "sayısal mahremiyet". It defines the right of individuals to determine by whom all the data they produce online will be collected and processed.

**Why is the "I have nothing to hide" argument flawed?**
Privacy is not about covering up crimes; it is a fundamental human right of individual autonomy, protection against manipulation and dynamic price discrimination, and the preservation of freedom of thought.

**What is the main difference between cybersecurity and digital privacy?**
Cybersecurity prevents data from being stolen by unauthorized third parties (protection against external breaches); whereas digital privacy prevents authorized platforms to which you entrust your data from profiling and selling that data without your consent.

**What do Differential Privacy and ZKP do?**
Differential privacy masks individual identities with mathematical noise in data analyses while measuring macro trends. Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKP), on the other hand, cryptographically prove the truth of a claim without sharing the information itself.
