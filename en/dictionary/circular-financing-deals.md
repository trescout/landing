# What is Circular Financing Deals?

*Development · Last updated: November 15, 2026*

It is a method where companies artificially inflate their sales figures by financing their own products through indirect financing channels.

## Overview

Circular financing transactions are a structural financial model created when a technology company conditions the other startups it invests in or funds to purchase its own products or services. This method can lead to the creation of paper revenue growth and make customer demand appear higher than it actually is. It particularly comes to the agenda in cases where capital circulates in the market and re-enters the same company as revenue, especially among artificial intelligence infrastructure and cloud providers.

***Analogy:** It is like lending the money in your pocket to your friend and then asking your friend to use that money to buy goods from your own shop. The total money in your pocket does not increase, but the shop's turnover looks high.*

## How it works

The parent company transfers funds to a subsidiary or business partner as venture capital or credit. The purchasing company spends a significant portion of this received fund to lease the parent company's cloud servers or processor chips. Thus, the spent money circulates around and is reflected back to the parent company as balance sheet revenue.

## Where it is used

It is encountered in venture capital rounds, high-cost artificial intelligence server rentals, and financial analyses examining inflated growth metrics in technology ecosystems.

## Commonly confused with

It can be confused with direct barter transactions. In a barter transaction, services are exchanged without cash flow; whereas in circular financing, actual cash movements are cycled under the guise of investment and sales revenue.

## Questions and answers

**Is circular financing illegal?**

Although not always illegal, it is subject to investigations by regulatory authorities in cases where it involves a lack of transparency and creates misleading balance sheet figures for investors.

**Why is it frequently seen in the technology sector?**

It stems from the need of startups and infrastructure providers to constantly demonstrate revenue growth in order to keep their valuations high.
