# What is Fingerprint?

*Dictionary · Data · Last updated: October 12, 2026*

> Device Fingerprint

It is a unique digital identity created through the user's device settings and browser characteristics.

## Overview

A device fingerprint is the combination of technical data collected to identify a user or hardware online without the need for cookies. It is calculated by bringing together dozens of parameters such as screen resolution, installed fonts, hardware architecture, and browser extensions. Even if cookies are deleted or incognito mode is used, these parameters do not change, ensuring detection with high accuracy.

***Analogy:** It is like immediately recognizing someone in a crowd by looking at their distinct features such as height, shoe size, hair color, and walking style, even if you do not know their name.*

## How it works

The visited web page runs a JavaScript script in the background. This script queries details such as graphics rendering performance (Canvas API), sound card responses, and the system time zone. All of this collected data is passed through a mathematical hashing function and converted into a single identity code.

## Where it is used

It is used in fraud detection for banking applications, preventing bot attacks, and in user tracking processes for advertising networks.

## Commonly confused with

It is confused with an IP address; while an IP address changes when you restart your modem or turn on a VPN, a device fingerprint remains largely constant as long as you use the same hardware.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does using incognito mode prevent fingerprint tracking?**

No; incognito mode only does not save browsing history and cookies, but it cannot change your hardware and screen characteristics.

**Is the creation of device fingerprints legal?**

It is considered legal for security and fraud prevention purposes; however, when used for advertising tracking, it requires explicit consent under privacy laws such as the GDPR.
