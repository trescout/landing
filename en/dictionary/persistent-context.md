# What is Persistent Context?

*Dictionary · AI · Last updated: October 4, 2026*

It is the ability of artificial intelligence models to remember and retain past conversations and important information with you, even after a session is closed.

## Overview

Persistent context enables artificial intelligence systems to store interaction history and shared data with the user over a long period. While models normally reset their memory with every new question, thanks to this technology they remember the details of previous conversations. Thus, you do not have to explain the same information from scratch every time.

***Analogy:** You can think of this like your neighborhood coffee shop that you go to every day, which greets you by name and prepares your coffee just the way you like it.*

## How it works

The system saves the conversations held with you and the documents you shared into a special database in the background. When you ask a new question to the artificial intelligence, the system automatically retrieves relevant past information from this database and appends it to the current question. This allows the model to generate a response by taking the history into account.

## Where it is used

It is frequently used in customer service bots, personal artificial intelligence assistants, and long-term research projects. This feature plays a critical role, especially in assistants that do not forget the general structure of a project during software development processes lasting weeks.

## Commonly confused with

It should not be confused with temporary memory (context window): While temporary memory only determines the word limit remembered during the current active conversation, persistent context preserves information even days after the conversation is closed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does persistent context slow down artificial intelligence?**

No, thanks to advanced caching and database technologies, information is retrieved very quickly, so it does not adversely affect the operating speed of the system.
