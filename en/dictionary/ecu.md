# What is ECU?

*Development · Last updated: November 16, 2026*

> Electronic Control Unit

It is the control unit that manages the engine and other electronic systems by processing sensor data in vehicles.

## Overview

Electronic Control Unit (ECU) is an internal embedded computer module found in modern automobiles and vehicles. It manages critical components such as the vehicle's engine timing, fuel injection, and braking systems in real time based on data coming from sensors. A vehicle can have multiple ECUs undertaking different functions.

***Analogy:** It is like the brain of the vehicle that manages all its muscles and organs, instantly reacting with reflexes based on stimuli from sensors.*

## How it works

It collects data such as temperature, speed, and pressure from sensors. The software on the internal microcontroller analyzes this data and sends commands to mechanical parts. All this communication generally takes place over in-vehicle network protocols.

## Where it is used

It is used in engine management systems, anti-lock braking systems (ABS), and airbag control units in the automotive sector.

## Commonly confused with

It might be confused with a single box that only controls the vehicle's engine. In modern vehicles, there are dozens of different ECUs that separately manage the engine, transmission, and comfort systems.

## Questions and answers

**Can ECU software be updated?**

Yes, manufacturer updates or performance-oriented software can be loaded into the ECU memory via authorized devices.

**How many ECUs are there in a vehicle?**

Depending on the technological equipment of the model, a vehicle can have anywhere from a few to over a hundred ECUs.
