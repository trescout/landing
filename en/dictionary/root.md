# What is Root?

It is the highest level authorized account that provides unlimited access to all files, settings and commands on an operating system.

## Overview
With a normal user account, you cannot change critical files of the system, but with root privilege you can get to the heart of the system. This power both provides full control over the device and carries the risk of completely disrupting the system in an incorrect operation.

*Analogy: In a hotel, it is like the hotel owner or chief technician has the key and electrical panel of all rooms, while the normal customer can only enter his own room.*

## How it works
It is done through a process called 'rooting' or 'obtaining root authority' on the devices. This process often requires bypassing the system's security locks.

## Where it is used
It frequently occurs in Linux-based systems and customization of Android devices.

## Commonly confused with
It is similar to the administrator account, but root generally refers to the deepest privileges in the system.

## Frequently asked questions
**Why doesn't everyone use root?**
Because the security risk is very high; If a malicious software gets root permission, it can take over the entire device.
