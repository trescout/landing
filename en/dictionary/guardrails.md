# What is Guardrails?

*Dictionary · AI · Last updated: October 9, 2026*

They are safety and control boundaries that prevent artificial intelligence models from producing harmful, misleading, or out-of-bounds outputs.

## Overview

Guardrails are programmatic control mechanisms that ensure artificial intelligence applications adhere to specific ethical, operational, and legal rules during user interaction. They monitor the prompts received by the model and the responses it generates in real time. By detecting risks such as harmful content, sensitive data leakage, straying off-topic, or hallucination, they block the response or frame it within a safe boundary.

***Analogy:** They are similar to steel barriers on the side of a curve. No matter how fast your vehicle is going, they physically prevent it from going off the road and plunging off a cliff.*

## How it works

Developers define specific rules, blacklists, and semantic controls. The user's request passes through an input filter before reaching the model; then, the response generated by the model is scanned by an output filter before being delivered to the end user. When the defined safety thresholds are exceeded, the system censors the response, returns a predefined standard error message, or forces the model to generate a safe response again.

## Where it is used

They are widely used in customer service chatbots, regulated sectors such as finance and healthcare, enterprise search engines, and autonomously operating artificial intelligence agents.

## Commonly confused with

It can be confused with Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) performed during the core training of the model. While core training determines the internal character of the model, guardrails are an independent safety shell attached to the model from the outside.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do guardrails systems significantly slow down response times?**

Additional control layers add a very small latency to the system, but thanks to lightweight rules and optimized smaller models, this delay is almost unnoticeable by the user.

**Do guardrails completely prevent prompt injection attacks?**

They are not a magic bullet on their own, but they catch a large portion of known vulnerabilities and command hijacking attempts, significantly reducing the level of risk.
